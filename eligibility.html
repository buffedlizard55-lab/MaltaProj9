01 · Eligibility rules

Can you qualify with no wages?

Potentially, yes. California coverage eligibility is not based only on whether you have a job. The county reviews the correct Medi-Cal category and household financial facts. This page identifies what official sources confirm and what is not known from the information provided.

All findings link to official California or federal sources.
Short answer: A U.S. citizen living in California may apply for Medi-Cal. For most adults under 65 who qualify through MAGI, California says the income limit is generally up to 138% FPL. Zero wages does not itself disqualify you. Your countable household income may include net rent, interest, and taxable gains; the county, not this guide, makes the decision. What to do with no paycheck

  1. Submit a Medi-Cal application anyway and accurately list all current/expected income sources.
  2. Use the expected income for the coverage year; update the application if estimates change.
  3. Respond to county requests and keep notices/contact details current.
  4. If ineligible for Medi-Cal, the same application checks Covered California plan and financial-assistance eligibility.

Careful distinctions: (1) Medi-Cal eligibility uses monthly MAGI against the 138% FPL standard, while Covered California credits use estimated annual MAGI—volatile trading income can satisfy one and not the other. (2) The separate 2027 work-rule $580 test is also MAGI-based monthly income (42 CFR 435.552(f)), applied to a qualifying month; DHCS’s preliminary “gross income” wording has been superseded on measurement. Passing the $580 work test never substitutes for meeting the Medi-Cal income limit, and vice versa.
