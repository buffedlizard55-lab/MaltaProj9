Income & MAGI

Three income streams, one measuring stick. For Medi-Cal and Covered California, everything is measured by household MAGI — and for the 2027 work rule, DHCS's materials allow income from any source to satisfy the $580 monthly standard. The federal tax character of your trading gains (section 1256 or otherwise) does not change either answer.

The MAGI math

MAGI = adjusted gross income + untaxed foreign income + non-taxable Social Security benefits + tax-exempt interest (SSI excluded). AGI already includes your taxable interest, net capital gains, and net rental income.

For your situation, the practical formula is:

MAGI ≈ net rental income (Schedule E) + savings interest + net trading gains (after capital-loss offsets, up to $3,000 against other income) + any other taxable income

Consequences worth planning around:

  • A big trading year can push MAGI over the Medi-Cal limit (138% FPL) or over the 400% FPL subsidy cliff — the marketplace would then be the path, possibly with no subsidy.
  • A loss year (rental losses limited by passive-activity rules; trading losses netted against gains) can drop MAGI back under the Medi-Cal limit.
  • Household means the tax household: you, a spouse if filing jointly, and tax dependents (with income of dependents who aren't required to file excluded).
  • From January 1, 2027, if you are in Medi-Cal's expansion group: monthly income ≥ $580 from any source satisfies the work rule for that month, per the DHCS outreach letter to members.

Kalshi specifics

What Kalshi itself reports (official company documentation): Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated Designated Contract Market. It issues 1099-INT (interest payments), 1099-MISC (credits/rewards), 1099-B (proceeds from broker transactions involving crypto transfers), and 1099-DA (digital-asset transactions through ZeroHash, computed FIFO), plus a monthly-updated Profit & Loss statement. It does not issue a comprehensive 1099 covering event-contract trading profit and loss, and states its information is not tax advice.

The open tax question (flagged): whether Kalshi event contracts qualify as section 1256 contracts (60% long-term / 40% short-term capital gain, year-end mark-to-market, Form 6781) has no IRS ruling either way; practitioners disagree and Kalshi takes no position. The Bipartisan Policy Center documented the uncertainty in August 2026. For healthcare eligibility this question does not matter: under any characterization, trading gains are unearned investment income that lands in AGI and therefore MAGI.

Trading hours as “work” (flagged): official state Medicaid materials say self-employment counts as qualifying work, but none addresses whether hours spent trading your own account qualify. Verified no-earned-income alternatives: the $580 income standard, 80 hours/month of community service, or at least half-time school. Confirm with your county before relying on trading hours.

Sources: Kalshi Help Center — Tax Documentation; IRS Form 6781; Bipartisan Policy Center issue brief (secondary). See Flagged for Review.

Rental income and the “property manager” question

  • Net rental income (rents minus expenses like mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, repairs, depreciation) is what lands in AGI and MAGI — not gross rents.
  • Rental real estate is generally a passive activity: losses are limited, though up to $25,000 of rental loss can offset other income if you actively participate (the allowance phases out at higher income), and qualifying real estate professionals are exempt from the passive limits.
  • If you are paid fees to manage property for other people, that is self-employment income (earned income). Managing your own rentals is reported as rental income. With $0 earned income, this research assumes the self-managed case — if you collect management fees, your filings (and possibly your work-rule posture) change.

Source: IRS Publication 527 — Residential Rental Property.
