Your situation: $0 earned income, property manager & Kalshi trader in California

You are a U.S. citizen and California permanent resident with no wages. Income comes from Kalshi futures/event trading contracts, rental property, and savings interest. You describe yourself as a property manager and full-time Kalshi trader. This page explains exactly how you can still obtain healthcare and how the 2027 work requirement can be satisfied with $0 earned income.

Research checked October 18, 2026 · Not an eligibility determination, tax, or legal advice · Every row below links an official source
Direct answer — you have three verified doors. (1) Medi-Cal today has no work test through Dec 31, 2026; eligibility is household MAGI ≤138% FPL ($22,025/yr for 1 person in 2026). Rental, interest, and trading gains already count toward that number, wages not required. (2) Medi-Cal from Jan 1, 2027 adds a community-engagement test for expansion adults 19–64, but it is not an earned-income test and in California it is not an every-month test — you need only one qualifying month per renewal period, which can be met with $580 of MAGI-based monthly income from any source. (3) Covered California has no work requirement at all; eligibility is based on estimated annual household MAGI. Losing Medi-Cal for noncompliance does not unlock subsidized marketplace coverage.

Exactly how you can fulfill the work requirement with $0 earned income

In California you need only one qualifying month per renewal period — not every month. At application, that month is the month right before you apply. At renewal, any one month since your last determination.

What does not count without written confirmation: hours trading your own Kalshi account or managing your own rentals. Federal “work” covers paid, in-kind, and unpaid non-community-service work and the CMS preamble + DHCS guidance include self-employment/business owners generally — but no official source addresses those two specific activities, and IRS Topic 429 says own-account trading gains are not self-employment earnings even for a full-time trader. That tax rule explains why earned income stays $0; it does not settle the Medicaid “work” question. Use the verified routes above and get written county confirmation before relying on those hours. Your action checklist — with $0 earned income

Step 1 — This year (before 2027)

Apply for Medi-Cal even with $0 wages

There is no work requirement in effect today. Apply any time via BenefitsCal, Covered California, or your county. The same application screens you for Covered California if MAGI is over Medi-Cal limits. Bring: estimate of annual household MAGI (net rent + interest + net trading gains), tax filing status, household members.

Sources: DHCS — Apply for Medi-Cal; Covered California — Medi-Cal & application

Step 2 — Pick your 2027 qualifying-month plan

Engineer one $580 MAGI month, or line up alternatives

If you expect a rental credit, interest posting, or trading realization to clear $580 of MAGI-based income in some month, keep monthly proof for that month. Otherwise secure a structured volunteer placement that tracks hours, or half-time school enrollment. Remember: you need only one month per renewal — but at application for 2027, it must be the month right before you apply, so time applications accordingly.

Sources: 42 CFR 435.552(f); WIC 14005.69(d)

Step 3 — Keep monthly proof

What to file by source

Kalshi monthly P&L + realized gains/losses, rent ledger (rents received and expenses — MAGI uses net), bank interest statements, volunteer log with organization contact, or school enrollment. Lump sums count only in month received (42 CFR 435.603). Ask the county how they budget recurring trading/rental amounts vs. one-time spikes.

Source: 42 CFR 435.603

Step 4 — Do not ignore mail

A missed letter can cost subsidized marketplace coverage

Open every Medi-Cal/county letter immediately and respond within 30 days. Losing Medi-Cal for noncompliance = no financial help through Covered California (full-cost plans only). If you disagree, request a fair hearing — coverage continues during the notice window.

Sources: DHCS ACWDL 25-30; DHCS MEDIL I26-25
