Other Options & Safety Net

These are secondary paths — backstops, or programs that apply only at 65+, with a disability, or after leaving a W-2 job. They are not substitutes for real coverage, but they matter in a gap year or a coverage transition.

Programs and protections

Other coverage options and safety-net protections
Item Value / Rule Details Status Source Notes
Emergency care (EMTALA) Screening and stabilization required regardless of ability to pay Hospitals with emergency departments that participate in Medicare must provide an appropriate medical screening exam and stabilizing treatment for emergency medical conditions (including active labor) to anyone, regardless of insurance status or ability to pay; payment questions cannot delay screening. Verified CMS - Emergency Medical Treatment & Labor Act (EMTALA)
Hospital charity care and discounts (California) Patients at or below 400% FPL eligible for charity care or discount payment policies California Health and Safety Code 127405 requires each hospital to maintain charity care and discount payment policies; uninsured patients or patients with high medical costs at or below 400% of FPL are eligible to apply; hospitals may not consider the patient's monetary assets in determining eligibility. Verified California Leginfo - Health & Safety Code 127405 Originally enacted via the hospital fair pricing laws; SB 1276 (2013-14) among amending legislation.
Short-term health plans Not available in California California Insurance Code 10123.61 (SB 910, Stats. 2018, ch. 687) prohibits issuing, amending, selling, renewing, or offering short-term limited duration health insurance in California effective January 1, 2019; federal changes to short-term plan rules do not override the state prohibition. Verified California Department of Insurance - Notice of Prohibition (SB 910)
Health care sharing ministries Not insurance; membership exempts from the California mandate penalty Health care sharing ministries are not health insurance; membership for at least one day in a month qualifies as a coverage exemption (code F) from California's Individual Shared Responsibility Penalty on Form FTB 3853. Verified FTB - Form 3853 instructions (2025) Sharing ministries do not guarantee payment of medical bills.
COBRA continuation coverage 18-36 months; up to 102% of the full premium Federal law lets workers and families keep job-based group coverage temporarily after job loss, reduced hours, and other qualifying events; the enrollee usually pays the entire premium plus up to 2%. Verified U.S. Department of Labor - COBRA
Medicare Age 65+ (or qualifying disability, ESRD, or ALS) Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months starting 3 months before the month you turn 65; some people are enrolled automatically from Social Security benefits. Verified Medicare.gov - Get started with Medicare Medicare enrollees are not subject to the Medicaid work requirement.
County Medi-Cal offices Applications, renewals, and work-rule determinations County welfare offices take Medi-Cal applications and make eligibility determinations; DHCS directs members to keep contact information current and respond to county letters quickly. Verified DHCS - Medi-Cal Changes Find your county office via the DHCS contact page.
Medi-Cal help and enrollment support County office, Covered California, or the DHCS Help Center DHCS offers a Help Center, county offices, and Covered California support for applications and questions. Verified DHCS - Qualify for Medi-Cal
Consequence of losing Medi-Cal for work-rule noncompliance No subsidized Covered California coverage - full cost of a plan DHCS ACWDL 25-30: 'Individuals who are eligible for MAGI Medi-Cal but fail to meet the work and community engagement requirements due to noncompliance will not be eligible for subsidized coverage through Covered California, even if they meet all other Medi-Cal requirements. In these circumstances, the individual will be evaluated for all other Medi-Cal programs.' The DHCS member outreach letter states the same: 'If you lose Medi-Cal because you did not meet these rules, you cannot get financial help to pay for a health plan through Covered California.' Verified DHCS ACWDL 25-30 (Dec 30, 2025) Also stated in DHCS MEDIL I26-25 member letter. This makes satisfying the requirement the highest-value action for anyone in the MAGI New Adult Group.
90-day cure period after a work-rule termination Return to coverage without a new application during the 90-day cure period; reapply at any time afterward DHCS ACWDL 25-30: existing members who cannot meet the requirement 'may return to coverage without a new application during the 90-day cure period once they provide information demonstrating compliance with the requirement. After the 90-day cure period, they can re-apply for Medi-Cal at any time.' Verified DHCS ACWDL 25-30 (Dec 30, 2025)
Required review of every other eligibility basis before a denial County must determine whether any other basis for Medi-Cal or another insurance affordability program applies, and must offer a fair hearing WIC 14005.69(k)(1)(E) and (n), as amended by AB 2161. DHCS ACWDL 25-30 Step 4 likewise requires the county to 'first evaluate the individual's eligibility for all other Medi-Cal programs that are not subjected to work and community engagement requirements.' Verified California Leginfo - AB 2161 (chaptered text)
Where to get help meeting the rule County Medi-Cal office, the DHCS work-rules page, the Medi-Cal Helpline, or BenefitsCal The DHCS member outreach letter directs members to their county office (dhcs.ca.gov/COL), the work-rules page (www.dhcs.ca.gov/workrules), the Medi-Cal Helpline at (800) 541-5555, and BenefitsCal.com. It also states: 'California lawmakers have invested in new resources to help you with these new rules' and that members will receive information on paid or volunteer work, job training, and schools before the end of 2026. Verified DHCS MEDIL I26-25 (Sept 11, 2026) dhcs.ca.gov/workrules resolves to the DHCS Medi-Cal Changes page.
BenefitsCal Official state benefits portal for applications, renewals, forms, document upload, and notifications The DHCS member outreach letter recommends creating a BenefitsCal account, describing it as 'the official website where you can keep track of your benefits and documents, complete forms, report changes, upload documents, read notifications, and more,' with opt-in text and email messages. The DHCS Apply page lists BenefitsCal and Covered California as the two official online application partners. Verified DHCS MEDIL I26-25 (Sept 11, 2026)
County office locator DHCS 'Find a county office' page The DHCS Apply page links the county office finder for phone and in-person applications, and the DHCS eligibility chart links the same page plus Covered California support and the Medi-Cal Help Center. Verified DHCS - Apply for Medi-Cal
Free enrollment help Covered California certified enrollers, county office, and the DHCS Help Center Covered California's open-enrollment page lists free help: 'Get Help - See all the free ways to get help, from live support to in-person enrollers.' Good to know

  • Emergency rooms cannot turn you away. EMTALA requires a medical screening exam and stabilizing treatment regardless of ability to pay — but it does not cover ongoing care, and bills can still follow you (see charity care below).
  • Hospital charity care is a right at qualifying incomes. California law makes uninsured and high-medical-cost patients at or below 400% FPL eligible for a hospital's charity care or discount payment policy, and hospitals cannot consider your monetary assets when determining that eligibility.
  • Losing Medi-Cal for the work rule also costs you the subsidy. DHCS states that an individual eligible for MAGI Medi-Cal who fails the requirement “will not be eligible for subsidized coverage through Covered California, even if they meet all other Medi-Cal requirements” — the county must still evaluate every other Medi-Cal program, but a marketplace plan would be at full price. (DHCS ACWDL 25-30; same statement in the DHCS member outreach letter)
  • There is a 90-day cure period after coverage ends. Existing members who cannot meet the requirement may return to coverage without a new application during the 90-day cure period, and can reapply at any time afterward (DHCS ACWDL 25-30).
  • Short-term plans are not an option in California. State law has prohibited them since January 1, 2019, and federal rollbacks of short-term rules do not revive them here.
  • Health care sharing ministries are not insurance. They can exempt you from California's penalty, but they do not guarantee payment and are not regulated as coverage.
  • Medicare removes the work question entirely — people entitled to or enrolled in Medicare are not subject to the Medicaid community engagement requirement.
