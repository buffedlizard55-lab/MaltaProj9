Work & Community Engagement Requirements — the New Law

Created by section 71119 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025), codified at section 1902(xx) of the Social Security Act, and implemented by CMS interim final rule CMS-2454-IFC (June 1, 2026; published June 3, 2026 at 91 FR 33348; effective July 31, 2026).

Who must comply Non-pregnant adults ages 19-64 not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare Applies to individuals eligible for or enrolled in the Medicaid adult group (the ACA expansion group) or in certain section 1115 demonstrations providing minimum essential coverage. CMS reports 43 states plus DC cover these populations and must implement; U.S. territories are not subject. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC These are called applicable individuals.
Monthly standard 80 hours per month (about 20 hours per week) Applicable individuals must demonstrate 80 hours per month of qualifying activities, or satisfy the income alternative, or qualify for an exemption. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC
Qualifying activities Work; community service; work program; at least half-time educational program; or any combination totaling 80 hours Individuals may combine activities to meet the 80-hour requirement. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC Federal definitions at 42 CFR 435.552(b): community service must be a structured public/nonprofit program that tracks hours; a work program excludes a stand-alone supervised job-search program (job search may only be a subsidiary activity under half the hours). Less-than-half-time school hours may be combined (credit hours x 3 x 4.33 weeks).
Income alternative (no hours needed) Monthly income of at least 80 x the federal hourly minimum wage ($580 per month in 2026) An individual can satisfy the requirement for a month with monthly income at or above 80 times the federal minimum wage instead of logging hours. Federal regulation 42 CFR 435.552(f)(2) measures that monthly income as the individual's MAGI-based income for their MAGI-based household (42 CFR 435.603) - so unearned MAGI income (net rental, interest, capital/trading gains) counts; California statute Welf. & Inst. Code 14005.69(e)(6) (AB 2161) uses the same MAGI-based method. DHCS member materials describe it as income from any source. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC Federal minimum wage $7.25 x 80 = $580. DHCS outreach letter: income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes). MAGI-based measurement re-verified 2026-10-18 against eCFR 42 CFR 435.552(f) and AB 2161 text; earlier DHCS 'total gross income' phrasing was preliminary (see FL-06).
Seasonal workers Average monthly income of at least $580 over the preceding 6 months The 6-month averaging route in federal law/regulation is for a 'seasonal worker' as described in Internal Revenue Code 45R(d)(5)(B): average monthly MAGI-based income over the preceding 6 months of at least $580 (42 CFR 435.552(g)). DHCS preliminary/member wording also describes averaging for people whose hours change frequently or with fluctuating income - whether mere income fluctuation (e.g., variable trading gains) qualifies without seasonal-worker status is flagged (FL-11). Verified 42 CFR 435.552(g) (eCFR) DHCS H.R. 1 Implementation Plan and MEDIL I26-25 describe averaging more broadly; do not assume variable Kalshi gains can be averaged - confirm with the county.
Self-employment counts as work Work = work for money, in-kind work, or unpaid work (other than community service); self-employment counts 42 CFR 435.552(b) defines work as work in exchange for money, work in exchange for goods or services (in-kind), and unpaid work other than community service. The CMS-2454-IFC preamble (91 FR 33348) states individuals need not be employees: people who work for themselves by starting/owning a business or as independent contractors meet the definition. DHCS ACWDL 25-30 likewise lists 'at least 80 hours of work' as including self-employment. Verified 42 CFR 435.552(b) (eCFR) Replaces the earlier out-of-state citation with the controlling federal definition + California ACWDL 25-30. Whether hours trading one's own account or managing one's own rentals are 'work' is still not addressed officially - see FL-02/FL-12. Trading gains are not self-employment earnings for tax purposes (IRS Topic 429), which is a separate question from the Medicaid work definition.

New applicants California: applicants - the month immediately before application; enrollees - any one month between determinations Federal framing: new applicants meet the requirement for at least one month before applying; existing beneficiaries for one or more months between renewals. California law (Welf. & Inst. Code 14005.69(d), as amended by AB 2161) fixes the California test: an applicant must demonstrate engagement for the one month immediately preceding the application month; an enrolled person must demonstrate it for any one month between their most recent (re)determination and the next scheduled redetermination. It is therefore NOT an every-month test in California. ACWDL 25-30 gives worked examples (e.g., one qualifying month out of six at renewal suffices; months need not be consecutive). Noncompliance process 30-calendar-day cure period, then denial or disenrollment; reapplication allowed at any time If a state cannot verify compliance it must send a notice of noncompliance and provide 30 calendar days to demonstrate compliance or that the requirement does not apply; failing that, the application may be denied or the individual disenrolled. Disenrolled individuals may reapply at any time and are assessed on reapplication. California adds: coverage continues during the 30-day period (and at renewal, active eligibility is maintained at least through the end of that month); notice is deemed received 5 days after its date; self-attested non-receipt is good cause; denial/disenrollment occurs no later than the end of the following month, only after checking every other basis of eligibility and offering a fair hearing (AB 2161, Welf. & Inst. Code 14005.69(k)-(n)). Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC No fixed lockout period appears in the CMS fact sheet. See also CA-14: California preliminary guidance allows return without a new application during a 90-day cure period after discontinuance once compliance is shown.

Tribal members, pregnant or 12-month postpartum members, and children are not subject. The rule is hours-or-income — not earned income

Nothing in the law requires earned income. A qualifying month is satisfied by 80 hours of qualifying activity (including structured unpaid community service), by at least half-time school enrollment, by any combination totaling 80 hours, or by MAGI-based monthly income of at least $580 (42 CFR 435.552(f)) — which DHCS's member outreach letter describes as income “from any source.” Net rental income, savings interest, and net Kalshi trading gains therefore satisfy the income standard without any wages. In California you need that showing for any one month per renewal period (new applicants: the month immediately before applying) — not every month. See Flagged for Review for the remaining open items: month-by-month trading documentation, six-month averaging scope, and trading / own-property-management hours.

Exemptions and deemed-compliant months

If an exemption applies, no qualifying activity is required. Some life circumstances make a month count automatically. States may also offer short-term hardship exceptions.

Verification and consequences

  • When checked: at application and at renewal (federally, states may check more often; California preliminary guidance checks only at application and six-month renewal, and not merely because you report a change). Counties must first try to verify from existing data (ex parte) before requesting paperwork.
  • New applicants (California): compliance is verified for the month immediately before the application month (the look-back month). If you first become compliant while the application is pending, aid begins the first day of the following month and earlier months are denied (ACWDL 25-30, preliminary).
  • Enrolled members (California): at each six-month renewal the county looks for compliance (or exemption/hardship) in any one month since the last determination — qualifying months need not be consecutive.
  • If the county cannot verify compliance: notice of noncompliance, 30 calendar days to respond with coverage continuing (deemed received 5 days after the notice date; self-attested non-receipt is good cause). Only after checking every other basis of eligibility and offering a fair hearing may the county deny or discontinue (no later than the end of the following month under AB 2161).
  • After termination: reapply at any time — no fixed lockout. California preliminary guidance allows former members to return without a new application within a 90-day cure period once compliance is shown. But: losing Medi-Cal for noncompliance does not unlock subsidized Covered California coverage — DHCS says financial help is unavailable and only full-cost plans remain (see WR-17/CC-13 in the tables above).
  • Six-month renewals: section 71107 of P.L. 119-21 doubles renewal frequency for expansion adults (California: from March 1, 2027).

Source: CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC; DHCS MEDIL I26-25.

Key dates at a glance

  • July 4, 2025 P.L. 119-21 signed (sections 71107 and 71119).
  • December 8, 2025 CMS initial guidance to states (information bulletin).
  • June 1, 2026 CMS interim final rule issued (effective July 31, 2026).
  • August 2026 DHCS begins mailing member outreach letters; texts began February 2026.
  • October 1 – December 31, 2026 Counties include the outreach letter with all new and returning applications.
  • January 1, 2027 Work and community engagement rules take effect; new applicants must show a compliant month before applying; retroactive coverage shortens to one month for childless adults.
  • March 1, 2027 Six-month eligibility checks begin in California; existing members are assessed at their first six-month renewal (the DHCS member letter tells current members the rules “will not go into effect until March 2027”).
  • October 1, 2028 Copayments begin for some adults (capped at 5% of household income).
