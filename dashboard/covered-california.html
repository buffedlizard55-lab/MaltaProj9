Covered California (Marketplace)

If your MAGI is above 138% FPL, Covered California is the primary path — federal premium tax credits for 100–400% FPL households, California state subsidies up to 200% FPL (2027), and reduced cost-sharing on Silver plans up to 250% FPL. Marketplace coverage has no work or hours test; eligibility runs on household income and access to other coverage. One critical exception: a person who is Medi-Cal-eligible but loses Medi-Cal for work-rule noncompliance cannot get Covered California financial help at all (full-cost plans only) — see CC-13 below.

How the subsidies work

  • Federal premium tax credit (PTC): available to households with income 100–400% FPL who buy through the marketplace and lack affordable other coverage. It caps what you pay for the benchmark Silver plan and can be taken in advance (lowering each month's bill) or at tax time. Because your income is rental + interest + trading gains rather than wages, nothing about the credit changes — MAGI is MAGI.
  • The 400% cliff is back: the enhanced credits that eliminated the cliff (and capped contributions) expired December 31, 2025 and were not extended by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Above 400% FPL ($63,840 for one person using the 2026 guidelines) there is no federal subsidy — a reason to watch realized trading gains late in the year.
  • California state subsidy: for 2027, $300 million was appropriated and eligibility extends to households up to 200% FPL ($31,920 individual / $66,000 family of four). Covered California projects 500,000+ recipients, with nearly 200,000 people able to choose a $0-premium Silver plan.
  • Cost-sharing reductions (CSR): if you pick a Silver plan and your income is 100–250% FPL, the plan variant is upgraded (Silver 94 / 87 / 73) with lower deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums. Above 250% and up to 400% FPL there is no reduction, because the statutory formula would push actuarial value past the 70% statutory ceiling (CMS 2026 payment-parameters guidance). For 2026 the reduced maximum annual limitation on cost sharing is $3,350 self-only / $6,700 other-than-self-only for the 94% and 87% variants, and $8,100 / $16,200 for the 73% variant.
  • Repay the difference: starting with tax year 2026, if your advance credit exceeded what your final income justified, the entire excess is repaid (P.L. 119-21 section 71305 removed the caps). Report income changes promptly — and note that capital gains realized in December can retroactively disqualify credits already taken.
  • No work requirement on the marketplace. Neither the federal premium tax credit nor Covered California applies any work, hours, or community-engagement test — this is an absence-based statement (the cited official pages list no such requirement); see Flagged for Review.

Medi-Cal, by contrast, is open year-round — and losing Medi-Cal because income rose is itself a qualifying event for marketplace special enrollment. Losing it for the work rule is not the same thing: it removes marketplace subsidy eligibility entirely.

The California penalty for staying uninsured

California requires residents to have qualifying coverage, claim an exemption, or pay the Individual Shared Responsibility Penalty with their state return: at least $950 per adult (and $475 per dependent child) or 2.5% of gross income over the filing threshold, whichever is more. Exemptions claimable on the state return include income below the tax filing threshold, unaffordable coverage, unaffordable family self-only coverage, coverage gaps of three months or less, health care sharing ministry membership, federally recognized tribe membership, incarceration, certain non-citizens, and enrollment in limited- or restricted-scope Medi-Cal. Since Medi-Cal and marketplace plans both count as coverage, the penalty mainly bites people who forgo coverage entirely.
