Income & MAGI

Three income streams, one measuring stick. For Medi-Cal and Covered California, everything is measured by household MAGI — and the 2027 work rule's $580 standard is itself measured with MAGI-based monthly income (42 CFR 435.552(f)), so unearned income from any source can satisfy it in a qualifying month. The federal tax character of your trading gains (section 1256 or otherwise) does not change either answer — and IRS rules explain why full-time trading plus self-managed rentals still produce $0 earned (self-employment) income.

Full-time trading and earned (self-employment) income Trading gains/losses for your own account are NOT self-employment earnings (no SE tax), even for a qualifying trader IRS Topic No. 429: a trader in securities buys/sells for their own account as a trade or business only if facts-and-circumstances tests are met (profit from daily movements, substantial activity, continuity/regularity) - calling yourself a trader does not decide it. The MAGI math

MAGI = adjusted gross income + untaxed foreign income + non-taxable Social Security benefits + tax-exempt interest (SSI excluded). AGI already includes your taxable interest, net capital gains, and net rental income.

For your situation, the practical formula is:

MAGI ≈ net rental income (Schedule E) + savings interest + net trading gains (after capital-loss offsets, up to $3,000 against other income) + any other taxable income

Consequences worth planning around:

  • A big trading year can push MAGI over the Medi-Cal limit (138% FPL) or over the 400% FPL subsidy cliff — the marketplace would then be the path, possibly with no subsidy.
  • A loss year (rental losses limited by passive-activity rules; trading losses netted against gains) can drop MAGI back under the Medi-Cal limit.
  • Household means the tax household: you, a spouse if filing jointly, and tax dependents (with income of dependents who aren't required to file excluded).
  • From January 1, 2027, if you are in Medi-Cal's expansion group: one month with MAGI-based income ≥ $580 satisfies the work rule for that renewal period in California (new applicants: the month before applying). Medi-Cal budgets MAGI monthly and counts a taxable lump sum only in the month received, while Covered California uses estimated annual MAGI — volatile trading income can therefore look different to the two programs; keep records month by month.

Kalshi specifics

What Kalshi itself reports (official company documentation): Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated Designated Contract Market. It issues 1099-INT (interest payments), 1099-MISC (credits/rewards), 1099-B (proceeds from broker transactions involving crypto transfers), and 1099-DA (digital-asset transactions through ZeroHash, computed FIFO), plus a monthly-updated Profit & Loss statement. It does not issue a comprehensive 1099 covering event-contract trading profit and loss, and states its information is not tax advice.

The open tax question (flagged): whether Kalshi event contracts qualify as section 1256 contracts (60% long-term / 40% short-term capital gain, year-end mark-to-market, Form 6781) has no IRS ruling either way; practitioners disagree and Kalshi takes no position. The Bipartisan Policy Center documented the uncertainty in August 2026. For healthcare eligibility this question does not matter: under any characterization, trading gains are unearned investment income that lands in AGI and therefore MAGI.

Trading hours as “work” (flagged): federal Medicaid “work” includes paid, in-kind, and unpaid work, with self-employment included per the CMS preamble and DHCS guidance — but no official source addresses hours spent trading your own account, and IRS law says own-account trading gains are not self-employment earnings even for a qualifying full-time trader (IRS Topic 429). That tax rule validates reporting $0 earned income; it does not settle the Medicaid hours question. Verified no-earned-income alternatives: the $580 MAGI-based income month, 80 hours of structured community service, or at least half-time school. Get written county confirmation before relying on trading hours.

Sources: Kalshi Help Center — Tax Documentation; IRS Form 6781; Bipartisan Policy Center issue brief (secondary). See Flagged for Review.

Rental income and the “property manager” question

  • Net rental income (rents minus expenses like mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, repairs, depreciation) is what lands in AGI and MAGI — not gross rents.
  • Rental real estate is generally a passive activity: losses are limited, though up to $25,000 of rental loss can offset other income if you actively participate (the allowance phases out at higher income), and qualifying real estate professionals are exempt from the passive limits.
  • If you are paid fees to manage property for other people, that is self-employment income (earned income) — and providing substantial services primarily for tenants' convenience moves rental reporting to Schedule C. Managing your own rentals is reported as rental income, which is generally not self-employment earnings. With $0 earned income, this research assumes the self-managed, no-third-party-fees case — if you collect management fees, report them as earned business income, after which the $0-earned-income premise no longer holds.
  • Whether documented hours managing your own units count as Medicaid “work” toward 80 hours is unresolved (FL-12) — do not assume; the verified path on rental cash flow alone is the $580 MAGI-based income month (using net rental income, so a depreciation-driven Schedule E loss month may not clear it even if gross rent does).
