Do I need earned income to get covered?

No. Both Medi-Cal and Covered California measure your household MAGI — which already counts your rental, interest, and trading income. Neither program requires a job or wages.

Medi-Cal →   Covered California →

Can I keep Medi-Cal in 2027 with $0 earned income?

Yes. The new work rule counts hours or income — not employment — and California tests any one month per renewal period, not every month. Meet that month with $580 MAGI-based income from any source (net rent + interest + trading gains), 80 hours of structured community service, half-time school, a work program, a combination, or an exemption.

Work requirements →

What is the new law, and when does it take effect?

Section 71119 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119–21, signed July 4, 2025), implemented by a CMS rule issued June 1, 2026. States must implement by January 1, 2027; California starts six-month checks March 1, 2027.

Key findings

  1. $0 earned income does not block coverage. MAGI — used by both Medi-Cal and the marketplace — counts interest, capital gains, and net rental income alongside wages. Income & MAGI →
  2. Medi-Cal today: adults 19–64 with household MAGI at or below 138% FPL ($22,025/yr for one person in 2026) qualify with no asset test, no premium, and no work requirement — nothing about work changes until January 1, 2027. Medi-Cal →
  3. The new work rule is not an earned-income test — and not an every-month test in California. From January 1, 2027, Medi-Cal expansion adults must show, for any one month per renewal period, 80 hours of qualifying activity, half-time school, a combination — or MAGI-based monthly income of at least $580 from any source — or an exemption. Failing it does not unlock subsidized Covered California coverage. Details →
  4. If MAGI is 138–400% FPL: Covered California has no work requirement at all — federal premium tax credits, state subsidies (up to 200% FPL for 2027), and Silver cost-sharing reductions (up to 250% FPL). Covered California →
  5. Enhanced federal subsidies expired December 31, 2025 — the 400% FPL cliff is back. A House-passed three-year extension (H.R. 1834) is stalled in the Senate as of this snapshot. Status →
  6. California's response: AB 2161 (signed September 14, 2026) requires counties to verify from data before paperwork, keep coverage alive during the 30-day response window, and check every other eligibility basis before cutting anyone. California rollout →

Timeline

  • July 4, 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21) signed; section 71119 creates Medicaid work & community engagement requirements; section 71107 creates six-month renewals.
  • December 8, 2025 CMS issues initial implementation guidance to states.
  • December 31, 2025 Enhanced ACA premium tax credits expire; the 400% FPL subsidy cliff returns for 2026 coverage.
  • January 8, 2026 House passes H.R. 1834 (three-year enhanced-credit extension), 230–196; Senate action still pending as of this snapshot.
  • June 1, 2026 CMS issues interim final rule CMS-2454-IFC (published June 3; effective July 31, 2026).
  • Aug–Dec 2026 DHCS mails Medi-Cal member outreach letters (from late August); counties include them with applications October 1 – December 31, 2026.
  • November 1, 2026 Covered California open enrollment for 2027 coverage begins (through January 31, 2027).
  • January 1, 2027 Work & community engagement rules take effect for Medi-Cal expansion adults; retroactive coverage shortens to one month for childless adults.
  • March 1, 2027 Six-month eligibility checks begin in California; existing members are assessed at renewal.
  • October 1, 2028 New Medi-Cal copayments for some adults (capped at 5% of household income) begin.
