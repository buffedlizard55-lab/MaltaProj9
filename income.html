03 · Income classification

Separate the money test from the work test

Your income can matter twice, under two different definitions: first for Medi-Cal income eligibility, and separately for the 2027 community-engagement test. An account balance, income earned on that account, property value, and rental profit are not interchangeable.

No dollar amount or tax classification is inferred where the official sources do not establish one.
Known from official sources: HealthCare.gov’s MAGI guide includes expected interest, investment income, capital gains, and net rental income. It treats self-employment as business income after expenses. DHCS says MAGI Medi-Cal does not apply property limits. For the separate 2027 compliance path, California guidance uses gross monthly income from any source, at least $580, and names dividends as an example. A taxable classification for each Kalshi contract and the DHCS calculation for your specific rent/interest is not established here.

What “no earned income” does—and does not—tell you

For Medi-Cal income eligibility

  • Use expected household income for the coverage year, not only wages.
  • Include the required household members and their income under the application rules.
  • List net rent, interest, dividends, and taxable gains as the relevant official income guidance directs.
  • County determines Medi-Cal eligibility; use actual tax records and current estimates.

HealthCare.gov: how to count income

For the 2027 community-engagement test

  • DHCS states monthly gross income of at least $580 from any source is one route—separate from working 80 hours.
  • For fluctuating income, DHCS describes a six-month average and includes dividends among examples.
  • If income is below the threshold, qualifying hours, part-time education, or an exemption may be relevant.
  • Do not assume an income source or trading activity counts without county confirmation.
