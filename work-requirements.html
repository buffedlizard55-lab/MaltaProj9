Work & Community Engagement Requirements — the New Law

Created by section 71119 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025), codified at section 1902(xx) of the Social Security Act, and implemented by CMS interim final rule CMS-2454-IFC (June 1, 2026; published June 3, 2026 at 91 FR 33348; effective July 31, 2026).

Monthly standard 80 hours per month (about 20 hours per week) Applicable individuals must demonstrate 80 hours per month of qualifying activities, or satisfy the income alternative, or qualify for an exemption. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC
Qualifying activities Work; community service; work program; at least half-time educational program; or any combination totaling 80 hours Individuals may combine activities to meet the 80-hour requirement. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC
Income alternative (no hours needed) Monthly income of at least 80 x the federal hourly minimum wage ($580 per month in 2026) An individual can satisfy the requirement for a month with monthly income at or above 80 times the federal minimum wage instead of logging hours. Self-employment counts as work Working includes self-employment, seasonal employment, unpaid work, and internships Official state Medicaid agency implementation materials list self-employment within the qualifying work activity. When compliance is assessed At application and at renewal; states may verify more frequently States verify compliance of applicable individuals at application, at renewal, and at state option at more frequent periodic intervals. Verified CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC

The rule is hours-or-income — not earned income

Nothing in the law requires earned income. A month is satisfied by 80 hours of qualifying activity (including unpaid community service), by at least half-time school enrollment, by any combination, or by monthly income of at least $580 — which DHCS's official member outreach letter describes as income “from any source” (before taxes). Rental income, savings interest, and Kalshi trading gains can therefore satisfy the income standard without any wages. See Flagged for Review for wording differences between official pages and the open question of whose income counts.

Exemptions and deemed-compliant months

If an exemption applies, no qualifying activity is required. Some life circumstances make a month count automatically. States may also offer short-term hardship exceptions.

Verification and consequences

  • When checked: at application and at renewal; states may check more frequently on their own schedule.
  • New applicants: must have met the requirement for at least one month before the month of application.
  • If the state cannot verify compliance: it must send a notice of noncompliance and allow 30 calendar days to demonstrate compliance or exemption. Only then may the application be denied or coverage terminated.
  • After termination: individuals may reapply at any time and are reassessed on reapplication — the CMS fact sheet describes no fixed lockout period.
  • Six-month renewals: section 71107 of P.L. 119-21 doubles renewal frequency for expansion adults (California: from March 1, 2027).

Source: CMS fact sheet on CMS-2454-IFC; DHCS MEDIL I26-25.

California's implementation

California is implementing the requirements for the MAGI New Adult Group. DHCS has been texting and mailing members since February/ August 2026, and the state enacted AB 2161 to add procedural protections. New copayments arrive October 1, 2028 at the earliest.

Key dates at a glance

  • July 4, 2025 P.L. 119-21 signed (sections 71107 and 71119).
  • December 8, 2025 CMS initial guidance to states (information bulletin).
  • June 1, 2026 CMS interim final rule issued (effective July 31, 2026).
  • August 2026 DHCS begins mailing member outreach letters; texts began February 2026.
  • October 1 – December 31, 2026 Counties include the outreach letter with all new and returning applications.
  • January 1, 2027 Work and community engagement rules take effect; new applicants must show a compliant month before applying; retroactive coverage shortens to one month for childless adults.
  • March 1, 2027 Six-month eligibility checks begin in California; existing members are assessed at their first six-month renewal (the DHCS member letter tells current members the rules “will not go into effect until March 2027”).
  • October 1, 2028 Copayments begin for some adults (capped at 5% of household income).

Note: CMS frames the deadline as “no later than January 1, 2027,” CRS describes “effective beginning December 31, 2026,” and DHCS's member letter says March 2027 for existing members — see Flagged for Review.
