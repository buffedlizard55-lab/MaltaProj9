What California is doing · no earned income required

California's help for people with $0 earned income

California's response to the federal work rule is not a new state requirement — it is the federal minimum plus state protections, plus free help to comply. Medi-Cal carries no premium, and the state also funds marketplace subsidies on top of federal help.

Includes AB 2161 (Chapter 209, Statutes of 2026, signed September 14, 2026) · Research checked October 18, 2026
What California did not do. California has not added a work requirement of its own. CRS reports that before Public Law 119-21 there was no statutory Medicaid work requirement comparable to the new one, and California's implementing statute (Welfare & Institutions Code §14005.69, as amended by AB 2161) sunsets with the federal law. DHCS implements the federal minimum through preliminary county guidance (ACWDL 25-30), which is expressly labelled preliminary and subject to federal regulations due July 1, 2029. Financial help beyond Medi-Cal

State subsidy on Covered California

For 2027, $300 million was appropriated (up from $190 million), extending state premium help to households up to 200% FPL — $31,920 for an individual and $66,000 for a family of four. Covered California projects more than 500,000 people will receive a state subsidy in 2027 (about 30% of enrollees), and nearly 200,000 can choose from two Silver-tier plans with a $0 premium.

Covered California, July 21, 2026

No member cost for Medi-Cal

Medi-Cal has no monthly premium. The only member charges DHCS lists are copayments beginning October 1, 2028 (above $0 and up to $35 per service, capped at 5% of household income, with exemptions for emergency, primary, prenatal, pediatric, family-planning, FQHC and rural clinic, and behavioral-health services) and premiums beginning July 1, 2027 tied to specific immigration-status groups.

DHCS Medi-Cal Changes; DHCS H.R. 1 Implementation Plan

Asset limits do not apply to MAGI groups

Under MAGI Medi-Cal, asset values are not a resource test — so savings and property ownership do not disqualify you. DHCS's own figures: non-MAGI Medi-Cal asset limits are $130,000 today and drop to $21,000 on July 1, 2027. That change applies to non-MAGI programs, not to the MAGI New Adult Group.

DHCS asset limits; DHCS Medi-Cal Changes

Hospital charity care

California law makes uninsured and high-medical-cost patients at or below 400% FPL eligible for a hospital's charity care or discount payment policy, and hospitals cannot consider monetary assets when determining that eligibility.

What California does not do

Consequences to be aware of.

Losing Medi-Cal for the work rule also costs the marketplace subsidy. DHCS states that individuals eligible for MAGI Medi-Cal who fail the requirement "will not be eligible for subsidized coverage through Covered California, even if they meet all other Medi-Cal requirements" — the county must still evaluate every other Medi-Cal program, but a marketplace plan would be at full price. Responding to a county notice of noncompliance within the 30-day window is therefore the single most important step. ACWDL 25-30; DHCS member letter
Guidance is preliminary and dates conflict. DHCS labels ACWDL 25-30 as preliminary, subject to change; federal regulations are due by July 1, 2029; and DHCS publications state the January 1, 2027 statutory start date in some places and March 1, 2027 in others. Six-month renewals also carry a statutory date of January 1, 2027 and a member-facing start of March 1, 2027. These are documented on the Flagged for Review page rather than silently reconciled.

Important: This page summarizes published official guidance; it is not an eligibility determination, and it does not decide whether any specific income source counts toward the $580 standard. Confirm with your county Medi-Cal office before relying on rental income, savings interest, or trading gains for work-rule compliance.
